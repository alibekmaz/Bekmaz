Enhancing landing page acquisition by improving the user experience

A redesign of Dadsun's landing pages and request flow, built on four years of behavioural data from the live product.

Short back story

In the last sprint meeting, we presented a 20% improvement in time on page and a 7% increase in CTA click rate after enhancing readability by simply changing the font size, font weight, and line spacing on our keyword pages. Then we received the unfortunate news of the IT section shutting down. Malek and I (designers) suggested a redesign of the landing pages and flows to apply everything we've gained and learned from Dadsun's user behavior. A proper farewell.

Let's start from flow

The old flow: registration first, then four service branches, each ending in a long form.

So logical! Let's add some human touch to it

· Are all this data that we get necessary?

We know that the easier the flow, the more likely people are to complete it. Our customer support team was already contacting users who submitted requests to boost the process, and Dadsun had paused the development of its specialized AI for automatic lawyer suggestions. We also found that users are more willing to share their data when interacting with customer support. All of these factors, combined with our goal to increase the request submission rate, led us to streamline the process by cutting unnecessary steps and focusing on just three crucial parameters:

City Category Type of service

· Order matters!

We decided to start with the easiest step for the user: selecting their city. It's something everyone knows and isn't sensitive information. After completing this simple first step, users are more engaged, leading to a higher likelihood of conversion.

City→ Category→ Type of service→ Registration/Login

· 1200 categories to choose in a search input? Let's refine it!

Since now this part is not in a form and has it's own step in the flow, we have the freedom to be more creative.

We used 8 general categories instead of 1200
Added an 'I don't know' option
Added a search bar for who knows

· Could type of services be more practical?

We observed many users struggling to choose the right type of service. Part of this issue originated from UX writing that wasn't easily understandable for the average person. Based on user behavior and our capabilities, we decided to:

AdvocacyConsultantContractsJudicial documents
AdvocacyConsultant before advocacyIn person consultantOn call consultant

Consultants were the best way to engage users, with many transitioning to different services afterward. Advocacy was the most beneficial service for Dadsun. We want to sell them better.
