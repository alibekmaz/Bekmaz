Intro

After setting my time management plan, I began exploring Trivago's platforms. While the UI wasn't as pleasant as I expected, the platform's consistency was good. I imagined myself as different types of customers: a poor student seeking cheap travel, a luxury traveler, a professional user needing efficient tools, and a disabled user facing challenges.

During this exploration, I encountered usability issues, poor information architecture, and unclear UX writing, especially on the result pages. Therefore, my first suggested improvement focuses on enhancing the components and layout of these pages.

Additionally, while using Trivago, I thought of an alternative flow to help users to choose the most suitable accommodation option. This idea arose from the overwhelming number of similar options, making it harder to decide. This new flow will be my second suggested improvement.

At the end I noted Some points and questions that could lead to possible adjustments in different parts, which I decided not to pursue in this case study.

The UI design in this case study focuses on desktop, I envision applying these changes to mobile; however, I haven't had enough time for that here.

SECOND SUGGESTION

A unique experience to find the best suitable option

To have the best impact in redesigning a service we need to diagnose the problem first. To do that, we need to draw user funnel. Let's assume the business goal is user visiting the hotel website by clicking on price CTA.

{{ f.pct }}
{{ f.text }}

If we want to improve our funnel we have two different options:

▪Improve conversion from step 1 to step 2 (low impact)
▪Improve conversion from step 2 to step 3 (high impact)

What makes it hard for users to choose? I think it's because of the "Paradox of choice". The paradox of choice is a phenomenon where an abundance of options can counterintuitively lead to less happiness, less satisfaction, and hamper the ability to make a decision.

How can we prove this theory?

▪Monitor user behavior on analytics tools.
▪Interview and asking questions from users who abandoned flow.

If we can prove this hypothesis and use the data we obtained from the interviews with users, we can redesign a new flow focusing on the simplicity of the selection experience.
